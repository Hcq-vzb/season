Household Chemical Filling & Packaging Lines

Detergents, sanitizers and disinfectants are filled on machines that look like beverage fillers but are specified very differently: the product's viscosity, foam tendency and corrosivity decide the wetted materials and the filling principle.

Four properties drive the specification

  • Viscosity: water-thin sanitizers and thick gel detergents need different filling principles.
  • Foam: surfactant products foam during filling, giving false levels and product loss.
  • Corrosivity: hypochlorite disinfectants attack many metals; wetted material selection comes first.
  • Containment: spills, fumes and operator exposure shape the layout more than speed does.

Weight filling for viscos products

Weight-based rotary filling is well suited to viscous household products because it measures net weight directly and tolerates viscosity variation between batches. Volumetric systems, by contrast, need re-calibration as viscosity and temperature shift.

Integration and changeover

Household chemical plants usually run several bottle formats and closures (trigger sprayers, flip-top caps, pumps). Filling and capping stations on a shared frame with servo adjustment reduce changeover time, and a multifunction block can combine rinse, fill and cap functions in one footprint.

Specify the wetted path against the real formulation — including fragrance and preservative components. Two detergents in the same category can need different seal materials.

Frequently asked questions

Can a beverage filling machine fill detergent?

Sometimes, but wetted materials and foam handling usually need changing. Detergent foams heavily and may attack standard seals, so nozzles, seals and the filling profile must be reviewed against the actual formulation.

Which filling principle suits viscous gel detergents?

Piston or volumetric filling handles viscosity well; weight-based rotary filling is preferred where batch-to-batch viscosity varies, because it measures net weight directly.

How is foam controlled during filling?

Fill from the bottom, use a two-speed profile (fast then slow), and allow a short settling time before capping. These measures solve most foam-related fill errors without changing the product.

Are explosion-proof configurations always required?

They are required when the product is flammable — alcohol-based sanitizers above the relevant flash point threshold. Water-based detergents and most disinfectants do not need them, but ventilation and containment still apply.
